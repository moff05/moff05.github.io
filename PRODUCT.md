# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters and hiring managers evaluating Nicholas Moffett for fall/spring internships and full-time roles at the intersection of commercial real estate and AI automation. They're scanning fast (5-30 seconds on first pass), comparing him against other candidates, and deciding whether to read further or move on. A smaller number of interested readers (people who click through, or come back after a first scan) will spend real time going deeper into project details and work history.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio site (nmoffett.com) that gets Nicholas hired. It needs to prove, credibly and quickly, that he's a real builder who ships (StackingPlanner, scholairly, internal AI automation at Goldenrod) and has genuine CRE domain exposure (SK Commercial Realty, Goldenrod), not just a student with a resume PDF turned into a webpage. Success = a recruiter remembers him, or clicks through to a live project and comes away convinced the work is real.

## Brand Personality

Technical, confident, personal. The voice is first-person and specific (he built X because Y happened at his internship), never third-person case-study-speak. Confidence comes from real shipped work and real numbers, not from design bravado — the site should read as someone who can build things, not someone who paid a studio to make them look impressive. Personal means the site can have some looseness and life (Off Hours section, off-brand fun) without undercutting the professional case being made in Work/Projects.

## Anti-references

Generic AI-generated portfolio sites — the current wave of near-identical Claude/GPT/Codex-coded portfolios. This is a real, live tension worth naming honestly: the site's current palette (cream/paper background, near-black ink, single coral accent, serif display + mono labels) is itself one of the most common AI-default aesthetics right now. It was a deliberate choice made earlier in this project, not an accident, and a full repaint is not in scope right now — but any further work should actively counter the *other* AI tells layered on top of that base (numbered section eyebrows, uniform card grids, generic motion, cliché copy patterns) rather than compounding them. Where the palette itself can't change today, the burden shifts to composition, copy, and interaction being specific to Nicholas rather than templated.

## Design Principles

1. **Prove, don't decorate.** Every design choice should make the real work more credible or easier to verify (working links, real screenshots, real numbers), not just look polished.
2. **First-person over case-study voice.** Copy reads like Nicholas talking, not like a template's placeholder structure filled in with his facts.
3. **Scannable first, deep second.** A 10-second scan must land the headline facts (what he builds, what he knows, what he's done). Depth is opt-in via click, not forced on every visitor.
4. **Restraint in structure, personality in detail.** The overall page structure should stay calm and legible; the "spice" belongs in specific, well-chosen moments (one signature interaction), not spread evenly as decoration across every section.
5. **Never assert unverified facts.** Standing rule from earlier incidents this project: no bio/achievement claim ships without being checked against a real source first.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG target set, but `prefers-reduced-motion` is already respected across the site's custom animations (hero reveal, involvement orbs, cursor effects), and interactive elements use visible `:focus-visible` states. Keep both of these intact in any future work.
